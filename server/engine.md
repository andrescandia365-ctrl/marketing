# The storytelling engine

This is CONTENT, not ads: stories people want to watch, that touch real everyday pain and show a real solution. It overrides the playbook's 75-second ad template; the playbook's moves, rules and guardrails still apply.

## Good against Bad
- The core device is a back-and-forth between THE GOOD and THE BAD. The Bad is the pain personified: it has a voice, a look, opinions and terrible excuses. The Good answers it. The story is their argument, until the Good wins by SHOWING, not by claiming.
- The viewer (or the person on screen) is still the hero; the Good is the guide that helps them win.
- Human characters (an owner, an employee, a family) carry the sincere moment.

## Three beats in every topic
- NEGATIVE: the pain, concrete and recognizable, played for laughs. Open on a real artifact with no setup (the empty shelf, the damp wall). Escalate: three beats of the same problem, each worse and more specific.
- TOUCHING: one sincere moment where the irony drops and we see what the pain costs a real person.
- GOOD: the solution working on screen, then the feeling after.

## Topics, bridges, callbacks
- A short runs 60–90 s and holds ONE topic.
- An episode runs 5–8 min in 3–4 topics of about 1:30–2:30 each; every topic can stand alone as a clip.
- Change topic on a concrete bridge (a date, a number, a time of day, a cut to a reporter "on location"), never a hard cut.
- Callbacks carry the point: plant a line or image early, pay it off later. The last line of the piece calls back to the first. In an episode, at least one callback crosses topics.
- A "correspondent" can report live from the scene and escalate the absurdity, then snap back to the real problem.
- The thesis arrives by callback, not by announcement.

## Loops: hook → retain → reward
- Hook: the first 1–3 seconds stop the scroll with a concrete pain or a question the viewer wants answered.
- Retain: keep at least one question open at every moment; each escalation opens a slightly bigger one.
- Reward: every loop that opens closes on screen, and the biggest one closes last. An open loop left hanging is a broken promise.
- The series loop (the brand's own routine, listed in the series file) is the product: when a piece shows a step, it shows the pain, the step doing its job and the result.

## Voice
- Rioplatense Spanish: voseo, plain everyday words, the way people talk in a corner store or at home. Short lines; about 2.5 words per second when spoken.
- Concrete beats clever: name the object, the number, the hour.
- Joke at the problem, never at the person or their home.
- End on relief or a doable action, never on despair. A soft close, not a sales pitch.

## Hard limits
- No politics. No real people's likeness. No copied shows, characters or catchphrases.
- Never invent statistics, testimonials or customer stories; mark anything uncertain [VERIFY].
- Never show a feature as real unless the series file lists it as working today; anything else is labeled concept.
- Never show the brand or a hero doing something illegal or against the series' rules (for example, selling an expired product).
